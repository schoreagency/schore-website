Branded search defends market share but rarely drives growth

Most DTC founders treat branded search as a safety net, but that mindset caps upside. The real question is whether you can turn that net into a launchpad.

2026-11-16 // SCHORE

Branded search locks in existing customers with minimal incremental cost

When a shopper types your brand name, the intent signal is the strongest you can get. CPCs are usually lower, quality scores higher, and conversion rates peak. In practice, a well‑structured branded campaign can shave 20‑30% off your CPA compared to generic terms.

That efficiency is why many teams allocate the bulk of their budget to brand keywords – they act as a defensive moat against competitors stealing traffic.

The defensive bias blinds you to growth opportunities within brand queries

The same intent that guarantees conversion also creates a ceiling. Users already know you; they aren’t discovering new products or categories. If you only bid on exact match brand terms, you miss out on related searches—brand + product line, brand + benefit, or brand + competitor comparison.

Data from typical DTC accounts shows that expanding to phrase and broad match variants can lift incremental impressions by 15‑25% while keeping CPA within a tolerable range.

Structure your brand campaigns to harvest upside without sacrificing protection

Separate defensive and growth ad groups. Keep exact match, high‑budget ad groups for pure defense—tight copy, single‑SKU focus, aggressive bidding. Create parallel ad groups that target phrase and broad matches, layer in dynamic keyword insertion, and test value‑driven copy that pushes cross‑sell or new‑launch messages.

Allocate a modest portion of the brand budget (10‑15%) to these growth ad groups, monitor incremental lift, and adjust bids based on ROAS thresholds. The result is a hybrid engine that defends share and nudges it higher.

// SCHORE'S TAKE

Branded search isn’t just a shield; it can be a springboard. By carving out dedicated growth ad groups and tolerating a slight CPA rise, you protect your base while pulling new revenue from the same high‑intent traffic. Treat brand keywords as a dual‑purpose asset, not a static defense.
