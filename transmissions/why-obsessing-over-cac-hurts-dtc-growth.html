Why obsessing over customer acquisition cost hurts DTC growth

Most founders treat CAC like a health metric—lower is always better. The reality is that CAC is a symptom, not a strategy.

2026-10-26 // SCHORE

When CAC becomes the north star, other profit levers get ignored

CAC is a lagging indicator of how much you paid to bring a user into the funnel. It tells you nothing about the value that user will generate once they convert. By obsessing over a single number, teams often cut spend on channels that actually drive higher lifetime value (LTV) because the upfront cost looks higher.

Illustrative data shows that brands that reallocate a modest 15% of budget from low‑CPC acquisition to higher‑LTV audiences can see ROAS lift by 20% while CAC rises modestly. The trade‑off is worthwhile when the downstream revenue more than offsets the acquisition expense.

LTV‑to‑CAC ratio, not CAC alone, predicts sustainable growth

The ratio of lifetime value to acquisition cost (LTV/CAC) captures the economics of a customer over time. A healthy ratio above 3:1 signals that each dollar spent brings back three dollars in profit. If CAC climbs but LTV climbs faster, the business actually improves.

Typical DTC brands that track LTV/CAC can afford higher CAC on premium channels like TikTok or paid search because the customers they attract stay longer, buy more often, and refer others. Ignoring LTV in favor of a static CAC target creates a false ceiling on growth potential.

Operational decisions should be driven by margin impact, not cost alone

Every acquisition decision should be evaluated against its contribution margin after accounting for fulfillment, returns, and post‑purchase spend. A cheap acquisition that yields a 5% margin is far less valuable than a pricier one that delivers a 25% margin.

When teams shift the conversation to “what does this channel cost us per order?” they miss the bigger picture: does the channel improve average order value, frequency, or referral rate? Those downstream effects are where real performance gains hide, and they are invisible if CAC remains the sole focus.

// SCHORE'S TAKE

Stop treating CAC as the holy grail. Shift your lens to LTV, margin contribution, and the downstream revenue it unlocks. When you measure what truly moves the bottom line, you stop chasing a number and start building scalable growth.
