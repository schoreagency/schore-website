Creative Fatigue

As ad spend increases, creative performance often decreases, a phenomenon known as creative fatigue. It can silently erode your return on ad spend, making it crucial to identify early.

2026-06-22 // SCHORE

definition and causes

Creative fatigue occurs when the same ad creatives are shown to the same audience repeatedly, leading to decreased engagement and conversion rates. This can happen for a number of reasons, including poor ad rotation strategies or insufficient ad budget to support the creation of new assets.

Typically, fatigue sets in after 2-4 weeks of continuous ad exposure, with click-through rates and conversion rates dropping by as much as 20-30% in the following weeks. This is especially true for e-commerce and DTC brands that rely heavily on social media advertising.

To make matters worse, creative fatigue can be difficult to spot, as it often manifests as a gradual decline in performance rather than a sudden drop-off.

identifying creative fatigue

So, how can you identify creative fatigue before it's too late? One approach is to monitor your ad performance metrics closely, looking for signs of decline such as decreasing click-through rates, conversion rates, or return on ad spend. You should also keep an eye on your frequency metrics, as high frequency can be a major contributor to creative fatigue.

Another approach is to use tools like ad rotation and creative testing to ensure that your ads are always fresh and engaging. By rotating in new ad creatives on a regular basis, you can keep your messaging from getting stale and prevent fatigue from setting in.

For example, a typical ad rotation strategy might involve introducing new ad creatives every 2-3 weeks, with a focus on testing different messaging, imagery, and calls-to-action.

prevention and next steps

Preventing creative fatigue requires a combination of strategic ad planning, continuous creative testing, and a willingness to adapt to changing market conditions. By prioritizing creative refreshes and rotation, you can stay ahead of fatigue and maintain a high level of ad performance over time.

If you're already experiencing creative fatigue, the best course of action is to take a step back and reassess your ad strategy. This might involve re-examining your targeting, ad creative, and messaging to identify areas for improvement.

In some cases, it may be necessary to hit the reset button and start from scratch, introducing entirely new ad creatives and messaging to reinvigorate your campaigns.
